Done: randvar (random_variable) answers a distribution object for every aide form. sample, randvector and randmultinomial draw from objects through the seeded generator. CAS-624 is done, master is pushed, and CI is green on 2488b71.
Summary: Xcas's random variables now exist: randvar turns a law's name and parameters, moment options or weight lists into one object that sample, randvector and later tasks' cdf, icdf, mgf and fitdistr read. A named law is its density's call with only its parameters, as giac prints it. The binomial and the discrete laws are randvar's own canonical call, so every object prints and reads back with no new kind of value. Every draw is seeded, so srand repeats a run. Two latent bugs surfaced on the way and are fixed: a giac-language reading of `mean(…mean=5…)` as a product, and a Simplify walk that went exponential on shared expressions once new builtins shifted symbol bits.

Decisions:
- binding: the distribution object (cross-task contract for T3, T5): a named law is the parameter-only density call that stays unevaluated, at exactly these arities: NormalPDF[μ,σ], ExponentialPDF[λ], UniformPDF[a,b], PoissonPDF[λ], GeometricPDF[p], NegativeBinomialPDF[n,p], StudentTPDF[ν], ChiSquarePDF[ν], FisherPDF[m,n], GammaPDF[a,b], BetaPDF[a,b], WeibullPDF[k,λ], CauchyPDF[x₀,γ]. The binomial law is RandomVariable[Binomial,n,p] (Binomial[n,p] is the coefficient). A discrete law is RandomVariable[{w…}] (draws indices 0…k−1) or RandomVariable[{w…},{v…}]. randvar leaves its canonical forms as they are (a fixed point), and randvar(X) of an object is X. Read objects only through `random_variables::variable(e) -> Option<Variable>` (Law(Law,&[Expr]) | Discrete{weights, values}) and draw through `draw(ev,&Variable)`. Routed: the module doc is the product doc; its unit tests pin it.
- Fits are exact on exact input (R67): normald(5,1), not giac's normald(5.0,1.0); uniformd(10−2√3, 10+2√3); binomial(162,1/9) as randvar(binomial,162,1/9).
- Rational weights are drawn exactly: integer masses over the common denominator, one integer drawn below their sum. Decimal weights are drawn in f64.
- randvector(n, multinomial, P) without V gives giac's counts per index (a list of len(P) naturals summing to n); with V it gives n elements.
- The warning texts are giac's messages verbatim ("Standard deviation must be positive", "Bad Argument Value", …), plus "too many draws" and "too many weights".
