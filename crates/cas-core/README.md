# cas-core

Hash-consed symbolic expressions for the CAS engine. The design is in Lific page `CAS-DOC-3`.

## Layout

| Module | Contents |
|---|---|
| `expr` | `Expr`, the 16-byte handle: a `Symbol` or an `i64` inline, or a shared interned node. Equality is O(1) (value or address) because every value has one representation. `Expr::apply`, `Expr::string`, `From<Symbol>`, `From<i64>`, `From<Integer>`, `From<Rational>` and `From<Complex>` (re-exported from cas-num): an integer that fits `i64` is always inline, a larger one an `Integer` node, a non-integer a `Rational` node, a rational with denominator 1 an integer, and a complex number a `Complex` node only when it is not real, so each number has one form. `view()` and accessors (`as_i64`, `to_integer`, `to_rational`, `to_complex`, `is_number`); cached metadata (`depth`, `size`, `may_contain` Bloom test, `is_numeric`, `has_pattern`); a structural hash. |
| `build` | The builder, the public way to construct expressions from Rust: `+ - * /` and unary `-` on `Expr`, `&Expr`, `Symbol` and `i64`, the assignment operators, `pow`, `Sum`/`Product` over iterators, `Expr::call`, `symbols`, `list`, `i` (the imaginary unit as a number) and `sin`/`cos`/…. Context-free: builtin `Flat` heads are flattened (iteratively) and `Orderless` heads sorted; nothing is evaluated (`x + x` stays `Plus[x, x]`). `x - y` is `Plus[x, Times[-1, y]]`, `x / y` is `Times[x, Power[y, -1]]`, and negating a number atom gives the negated literal (`-i64::MIN` is the integer `2^63`). |
| `symbol` | `Symbol`: a permanent `u32` id into a global, append-only name table, which starts with the builtins. |
| `builtin` | The builtin symbols (`Symbol::PLUS`, `Symbol::BLANK`, …): fixed ids `0..COUNT` and fixed attributes, declared in one list. Every name is an ASCII capital, then ASCII letters and digits, so the language's escaped user names (`$E`) never meet one. `I` is the imaginary unit's symbol, a constant that evaluates to the complex number, and `Complex` the head of complex numbers in full form. The list also holds the heads the language lowers to (CAS-120): comparisons and logic, `If`, `Function`, `Range`, `At`, the infinities, the statement heads `Set`, `SetDelayed`, `UpSetDelayed`, `Assume` and `Clear`, and the type tests of patterns; most of their evaluation comes with the Rewriting tickets. CAS-54 adds `SameQ`, `FreeQ`, `Length`, `Part`, `Map` and `Apply`; CAS-58 the sign predicates `Positive`, `Negative`, `NonNegative` and `NonPositive`, `Abs`, and the number sets `Integers`, `Rationals`, `Reals` and `Complexes`. Which heads are pattern constructs. |
| `attributes` | `Attributes`, the attribute bit set (`FLAT`, `ORDERLESS`, `HOLD_ALL`, `NUMERIC_FUNCTION`, …). |
| `assume` | Assumptions and the number domain (CAS-DOC-4 §7, CAS-58). `assuming` reads each assumption into facts once, evaluating the differences of its comparisons in the parent: comparisons, `Positive` and its kin, `And` and `Not` of them bound a *subject* (an expression as `scale·subject + shift` with the subject monic, so `x > y + 1`, `2 x - 2 y > 2` and `y - x < -1` all bound `x - y`), `Unequal` excludes a value, `Element` gives a number set, and anything else holds as written. A `Range` is what is known of a value: real or not, bounds with exact rational ends, not zero: from the facts about its subject and its structure (numbers are points, `Pi` and `E` lie between rationals, sums add ranges, coefficients scale them, products and powers combine signs, `Abs` is not negative, `Exp` of a real is positive). In the Real `Domain`, the default, symbols without facts are real. `Context::decide` (`Truth`) and `Context::sign` (`Sign`); a comparison reads the range of the difference of its sides, evaluated when neither is a rational, so `x + 1 > x` holds. Sound and cheap, not complete. |
| `context` | `Context`: values, rules, attributes of user symbols, assumptions (as written, and their facts), the number domain (`set_domain`) and limits. Values, attributes and rules are persistent maps keyed by symbol (`symbol_map`), so a clone is O(1) and a change to a shared state copies O(log n) of one map; `assuming` returns a child scope. `set` refuses a symbol with up-rules (`ContextError::HasUpRules`, O6), and `set_attributes` recompiles the rules that depend on the symbol (`rules`). Every state has a globally unique `StateId`, which keys the per-node fixpoint stamp (`is_fixpoint`, `mark_fixpoint`). `Limits`: `recursion` (512 by default, safe on a 2 MiB stack), `iterations` (4096), `match_steps` (10⁶ steps per match attempt, then `EvalError::MatchLimit`) and `stack` (0, the caller's thread); `Limits::deep()` asks for a thread of its own with 256 MiB of stack and the recursion it holds (about 8.7·10⁴ levels). |
| `eval` | `Context::eval` (CAS-DOC-4 §2, CAS-53): numbers and strings are themselves and a symbol with a value its value; a node stamped as a fixpoint of the state, or met before in the call (the memo, keyed by node identity), is done at once; otherwise the head and the arguments not held (`HoldFirst`, `HoldRest`, `HoldAll`) are evaluated, `Sequence` arguments spliced (in held positions too), a `Flat` head flattened, a `Listable` head threaded over lists (lists of different lengths, or a list with a scalar, are `EvalError::Shape`: no broadcasting, O8), and an `Orderless` head's arguments sorted (in `Times`, lists keep their order after the other factors). Then the first rewrite source applies (R10): the up-rules of the arguments, the head's native function (`NativeFn` for a builtin, `UserNative` for a user's symbol), the system rules of a builtin or the rules of a user's symbol (exact ones first), and for a compound head its sub-rules and a `Function` (`rules`). A symbol with a value is replaced by it, and `I` has the value `i`. A change starts over and counts against `Limits::iterations`; a fixpoint is stamped. Statements (`Set`, `SetDelayed`, `Clear`) stay unevaluated. `Eval`, the state of one call, is what native functions receive. Each nested evaluation is a level of `Limits::recursion`; a level takes at most `STACK_PER_LEVEL` (3072) bytes of stack (measured through arguments, heads, natives that evaluate their arguments, the matcher in order, as a multiset and in runs, and rules whose conditions recurse: at most 2672 in debug, 672 in release), so `RecursionLimit` comes before an overflow. Definitions, `matches` and `replace_all` run on the evaluation thread too (`run`). A `Function` head is applied in step 6 (`pattern::apply_function`). `Times` and `Power` are not `Listable`: `*` is the product of linear algebra (R13). |
| `native` | The natives of the builtins (CAS-DOC-4 §6.1, CAS-54), each receiving the normalised application. `Plus` adds its numbers, collects like terms in one pass (they are adjacent in the canonical order), drops zeros and unwraps one term; `Times` multiplies its numbers, adds the exponents of equal bases (`x x^(-1)` is 1), multiplies powers of numbers with one exponent (`2^(1/2) 3^(1/2)` is `6^(1/2)`), makes `0 x` 0 and leaves lists to R-10. `Power`: exact numbers to integer powers (estimated results over 2²⁴ bits stay), `x^0`, `x^1`, `1^x`, `(b^p)^n` and `(x y)^n` for an integer `n`, `(b^p)^q` for a positive rational `b`. Rational powers of rationals (`radical`) take a canonical form: a rational coefficient times powers of positive integers with exponents in (0, 1), one per exponent, perfect powers taken out per prime by trial division below 2¹⁶ and perfect-power tests (`12^(1/2)` is `2·3^(1/2)`, `2^(-1/2)` is `2^(1/2)/2`, `(2/3)^(1/2)` is `6^(1/2)/3`), square roots of negatives imaginary, other roots of negatives left for the number domain (CAS-58). `Sqrt[x]` is `x^(1/2)`. Infinities (`number::Infinity`): `DirectedInfinity` normalised to a direction of modulus 1 (`Infinity` for 1, `ComplexInfinity` for none), absorbing finite terms, turned by a coefficient's sign, and `Indeterminate` for `∞ - ∞`, `0·∞`, `0^0`, `∞^0`; `1/0` is `ComplexInfinity`. Comparisons decide on numbers, `±Infinity` and structurally equal expressions, and otherwise as the assumptions and the domain tell (`assume`); so do `Positive`, `Negative`, `NonNegative`, `NonPositive` and `Element`. `SameQ`, `FreeQ` and the type tests always decide: `IntegerQ`, `RationalQ`, `RealQ` and `ComplexQ` are true of a value the assumptions put in the set, `NumberQ`, `ListQ`, `MatrixQ`, `StringQ` and `SymbolQ` are structural. `Abs` of numbers (a complex one's is the square root of its norm), of infinities, of known signs, and of numeric coefficients. In the Real domain, odd roots of negatives are real (`(-8)^(1/3)` is -2, O7), `(b^p)^q` is `b^(p q)` for a real `b`, an integer `p` and an odd denominator of `q`, and `Abs[b]^(p q)` for an even `p` (`(x^2)^(1/2)` is `Abs[x]`); for `b >= 0`, `b^(p q)` in either domain; a directed infinity times factors of known sign turns by it; `And` and `Or` short-circuit, `Not` and `If` choose. `Length`, `Part` (0 the head, negative indices from the end), `Map` and `Apply`. `ReplaceAll` (from the top, a rewritten part not looked into again), `Replace` (the whole expression), `ReplaceRepeated` (until nothing changes, each round counted against `Limits::iterations`) and `MatchQ` compile their rules on each call, in the evaluation's context. In `Times`, patterns that match only lists (`_List`, `?MatrixQ`) keep their place with the lists, so a rule for `y x` keeps its order. `Plus` and `Times` first check, without building anything, whether the application is already normal, the usual case; debug builds check every such answer against the full computation. |
| `pattern` | Patterns and rules (CAS-DOC-4 §3, CAS-55). `Pattern::new` compiles `Blank`, `BlankSequence`, `BlankNullSequence`, `Pattern`, `Alternatives`, `Optional` (with the defaults of `Plus`, `Times` and the exponent of `Power`), `Condition`, `PatternTest` and `HoldPattern` into a tree of match nodes; anything else, and any part without a pattern construct, is a literal. It numbers the variables (slots), records the literal symbols every match contains as a Bloom filter in the format of the node metadata (so a subject without them is rejected at once), and lists the user heads it mentions, whose attributes matching depends on. The matcher backtracks through continuations, so every way of matching is tried, in order, until one completes: sequences shortest first, alternatives from the first, a repeated name bound to the same expression (O(1)); each node takes a level of the recursion limit while it is on the stack. The attributes a head has when the pattern is compiled decide how its arguments match (CAS-57): in order; under `Flat` in runs, a run of several bound wrapped in the head; under `Orderless` as a multiset (`multiset`), the arguments taking subjects in the order literals, fixed heads, blanks, applications a lone expression may stand for, sequences, a node whose value is known (a literal or a variable bound already) taking only an equal subject, equal subjects tried once, the last node taking what is left; under both, groups of several. With `OneIdentity`, a lone expression stands for an application whose arguments may all be missing but one (`a_. x_` matches `y`, `x_^n_.` matches `y`). In `Times`, lists are taken in the pattern's order. `Pattern::with_context` and `Rule::with_context` use a context's attributes and defaults, `Pattern::new` and `Rule::new` the builtins' only; definitions and the `Replace` natives compile in the evaluation's context. One match attempt takes at most `Limits::match_steps` steps (`EvalError::MatchLimit`). `substitute` instantiates a right-hand side but never inside a `Function` that binds a variable, so closures keep their parameter. `Rule::new` compiles `lhs -> rhs` once for reuse; a right-hand side `Condition[rhs, test]` is checked inside the match, so a failing test backtracks to the next way of matching. `Context::matches` and `Context::replace_all`. `apply_function` applies `Function[List[x, …], body]`, `Function[x, body]` and slot functions `Function[body]` (`Slot[n]`, not inside a nested slot function). `Pattern::with_defaults` takes the defaults of user heads, and `Pattern::filter` gives the dispatch filter of a rule (CAS-56). |
| `rules` | The rule store and dispatch (CAS-DOC-4 §5, CAS-56). Each symbol has its `SymbolRules`: an exact table for ground left-hand sides, keyed by the left-hand side with its arguments evaluated and normalised (`f[1 + 1] = 3` is the rule for `f[2]`; the arguments of an `Orderless` head sorted); patterned down-rules in definition order, where a rule with the same left-hand side and condition up to the names of its variables replaces the old one in place; the up-rules it carries for other heads, tied to an atomic argument, an argument's head or a blank's head (`f /: g[_f]`); sub-rules for `f[…][…]` on the innermost head; a native function; and the default of `x_.`. `Context::define` (`Set`: the right-hand side evaluated now), `define_delayed`, `define_many` (a library, all or nothing, on one evaluation thread), `define_up`, `define_up_delayed`, `clear` (attributes and default stay), `define_native` and `set_default`; protected symbols take none, a symbol with a value takes no up-rule and one with up-rules no value (O6). Each patterned rule has a filter on its subject's head, number of arguments and first argument, read from the compiled pattern. Changing the attributes or the default of a symbol recompiles the rules that mention it, found through an index: their stored left-hand sides are sorted and flattened again without evaluating, and definitions that become equal collapse as if made again in order. The system rules of the builtins are one static set, empty until CAS-59. |
| `symbol_map` | `SymbolMap`, a persistent 32-way trie keyed by symbol id: a lookup follows one path, and an update copies the nodes on its path only where they are shared. |
| `order` | The canonical order, `impl Ord for Expr`: numbers first, integers of any size and rationals by value, then complex numbers by real part and imaginary part; products and powers compared as products of powers (factors from the last, then the coefficient), giving `1 + 2 x + x^2 + 2 y + 2 x y + y^2`; then strings, symbols by name (case-insensitive, lowercase first; a `$`-escaped user name, `$E` for the language's `E`, sorts as its spelling, just before it) and applications by head, length and arguments. Independent of ids, hashes and addresses. |
| `print` | Printing. `Debug` and `Expr::full_form` give the unambiguous full form, `Plus[x, Times[2, Power[y, 3]]]`; `Display` gives infix, `x + 2*y^3`. Infix parenthesises only where precedence needs it, subtracts terms with a negative coefficient, makes negative integer exponents a denominator (`x/(y*z^2)`), never lets an operand start with a sign after an operator (`2*(-3)`, `(-3)^2`, `x^(-1)`, `-(-x)`), and keeps `Times[-1, 3]` apart from the integer `-3` (`-1*3`). A rational prints `3/2` (full form too) and binds like a quotient (`x^(1/2)`, `2*(3/4)`, `1/2*x`); `Times[3, Power[2, -1]]` prints `3*2^(-1)` so it cannot be read as the rational. A complex number prints `2 + 3*I`, `3*I` or `I` and binds like that sum, product or atom (`x*(2 + 3*I)`, `(3*I)^2`, `x - I`); in full form it is `Complex[2, 3]`, apart from the symbol `I`. Both printers run on an explicit stack, so any depth prints in constant native stack. |
| `read` | `Expr::parse_full_form`, the inverse of the full-form printer (CAS-52): `head[args…]`, compound heads, integers of any size and reduced rationals, strings with the printer's escapes (`\"`, `\\`, `\n`, `\t`, `\r`, `\u{…}`), symbols whose names are identifiers (a letter, `_` or `$`, then letters, digits, `_` and `$`, so `$E` reads back), and `Complex[re, im]` of two numbers as the complex number. Whitespace between tokens is ignored, nothing is evaluated or normalised, and errors (`FullFormError`) carry a byte position. It runs on an explicit stack, so any depth reads in constant native stack. `assert_eval!(ctx?, input, expected)` states evaluator tests in full form (CAS-DOC-4 §10). |
| `traverse` | Structural traversal, never evaluating. `part`, `Index` and `replace_part` use the `Part` convention (`0` is the head, `i ≥ 1` the arguments). `positions`, `pre_order` and `post_order` visit every occurrence. `distinct`, `symbols`, `contains`/`free_of`, `replace` (top-down), `substitute` (simultaneous literal rules) and `map_bottom_up` visit each distinct subexpression once, memoised by node identity, so they are linear in distinct subexpressions on shared DAGs. Unchanged parts keep their handle. `contains` and `substitute` skip subtrees by their metadata. All of it is iterative. |
| `node` | Interned nodes (integers outside `i64`, non-integer rationals, strings and applications) with their hash and metadata. Dropping releases children iteratively through a per-thread queue, so deep expressions use constant stack. |
| `intern` | The global interner: 64 mutex-protected shards of weak references, looked up with a borrowed key (a hit allocates nothing). A node removes its own entry when dropped, matched by address; shards shrink when sparse. `interner_stats()` reports occupancy. |

## Testing

Unit tests are in `src/tests.rs`. They cover structural equality against a plain tree model (proptest, with big integers, rationals and complex numbers among the leaves), one form per number at the `i64` boundaries whatever the constructor and for complex numbers with a zero imaginary part, sharing, entry removal, the builtin table and the `NUMERIC`/`HAS_PATTERN` flags (also against the model), constant-stack drops 10⁶ deep, and concurrent construction, churn and symbol creation. The rare interleavings (full hash collisions, a dead and a live entry for the same contents) are set up deterministically on a private shard. Context tests are in `src/context/tests.rs`: protection, scopes that don't leak, ids unique across threads, and a proptest that a stamp matches only the state that wrote it. Order tests are in `src/order/tests.rs`: polynomial and name examples, independence from creation order, and proptests of antisymmetry, transitivity and `Equal` exactly for equal expressions. Builder tests are in `src/build/tests.rs`: every operand type, constant-stack flattening 10⁵ deep, and proptests that `Plus`/`Times` match a flatten-and-sort model, are idempotent and ignore argument order, and that the operators agree with `Expr::call`. Printing tests are in `src/print/tests.rs`: precedence snapshots (negative numbers, nested unary minus, quotients, big integers and rationals, full-form fallbacks, string escapes), constant-stack printing 10⁵ deep, and proptests. Those parse the infix output back with a strict test parser, which rejects an operand that starts with a sign, and check that printing again gives the same text and the same numeric value. They also check full form against a recursive model. Reader tests are in `src/read/tests.rs`: symbols, numbers at the `i64` boundaries, complex numbers and the applications of `Complex` that are not numbers, every escape, compound and non-symbol heads, whitespace, every error with its position, text 10⁵ deep, `assert_eval!`, and proptests that full form reads back as the expression it was printed from (with or without whitespace between tokens) and that any text either fails cleanly or reads as something that reads back. Evaluation tests are in `src/eval/tests.rs`: atoms and values, holds, statements left unrun, `Sequence`, `Flat`, `Listable` (and its shape errors), `Orderless` (lists in `Times`), native functions and the evaluation of their results, the memo (a repeated part evaluated once per call), stamps (a second evaluation calls nothing), the iteration and recursion limits on the caller's thread and on a thread of its own (10⁵ levels), the stack each level takes through arguments, heads, natives, the matcher and rules against `STACK_PER_LEVEL` (run in debug and release), and proptests of idempotence, of results with and without stamps and memo, and of argument order under `Orderless` heads. `benches/eval.rs` times evaluating a sum of 10³ to 10⁵ terms (linear, about 6.7·10⁶ terms/s) and evaluating the result again (22 ns at every size: the stamp). Native tests are in `src/native/tests.rs`: tables of sums, products, powers, radicals, infinities and the singular cases, comparisons and predicates, logic and conditionals (with a test that short circuits evaluate nothing more), and structure; proptests that arithmetic on numbers agrees with cas-num, that rational powers keep their numeric value in a form that evaluates to itself, that perfect powers come out, and that the order of the arguments of `Plus` and `Times` changes nothing. Pattern tests are in `src/pattern/tests.rs`: tables for blanks and bindings, sequences (fewest first, bindings that must agree across nested parts, splicing), alternatives, `Optional` and the builtins' defaults, conditions, tests, `HoldPattern`, `Replace`, `ReplaceAll` and `ReplaceRepeated` (with a right-hand condition that backtracks), `Function` with names and slots; closures that keep their parameter; a compiled rule reused across contexts against compiling it again; malformed patterns; and proptests that the matcher finds a match exactly when a brute-force reference does, with the same first bindings, and that the symbol filter changes no result. Tests of `Flat`, `Orderless` and `OneIdentity` matching are in `src/pattern/multiset/tests.rs`: tables for each, the lists of `Times`, rules on such heads through dispatch, the budget (deterministic, `MatchLimit`), pruning that keeps easy matches within a small budget, and a proptest against a brute-force oracle over pattern expressions (no pruning, every copy of equal subjects tried) on existence and on the first match, half of the cases from patterns made from their subjects so that a third match. `benches/matching.rs` times linear cases (a split, a literal removed, a fixed head among 10⁴ terms: 35 to 50 ns per term) and the exponential one, three sequences under an `Orderless` head (about ×10 for two more arguments), up to where the budget stops it (12 arguments: 0.19 s, about 190 ns per step). Assumption tests are in `src/assume/tests.rs`: tables in both domains (signs, real roots, powers of powers, `Abs`, `Element`, the type tests, bounds and their implications, facts about differences in any form, exclusions and number sets, negated facts, assumptions that hold as written, scopes and the API, rule conditions that see the facts), and a proptest that `decide` and `sign` never contradict exact evaluation at points that satisfy random assumptions (bounds of either strictness, exclusions, both sides), in both domains. Rule tests are in `src/rules/tests.rs`: exact keys evaluated and normalised, definition order after the exact rules, replacement in place by key (variable names, conditions, literals that share a variable's name), `Set` against `SetDelayed`, recursion through rules on the caller's thread and on a thread of its own, sub-rules, up-rules before natives and on blanks' heads, the O6 exclusions, protection, `clear`, user natives, defaults, recompilation after an attribute or default change (rules of `g` and `h` that mention `f`), held left-hand sides, `define_many` all or nothing, persistence, malformed definitions and the system rules; proptests that setting attributes after the definitions gives the rules and the results of setting them before, and that the dispatch filters change no result. `benches/rules.rs` times dispatch through 10 to 10⁵ exact rules (flat: 0.11 µs, 1446 instructions, per evaluation), through 10 and 100 patterned rules that the first-argument filter tells apart (0.22 and 0.40 µs: about 50 instructions per rule skipped) or that the matcher must try (0.57 and 4.0 µs: about 38 ns per attempt), loading 10³ to 10⁵ definitions (linear: about 0.18 µs per exact definition and 1.1 µs per patterned one, and O(log n) more when every state is kept, 1.4 to 2 µs), and evaluating a sum of 10⁴ terms with and without an up-rule elsewhere (0.75% more with one: an application whose Bloom filter has none of the tags skips up-rules in O(1)). The map has a proptest against a `BTreeMap`, with a snapshot that later changes must not reach. Traversal tests are in `src/traverse/tests.rs`: proptests against recursive models (orders, distinct, positions and parts, `contains`, `symbols`, `replace`, `substitute`, `map_bottom_up`); a 2¹⁰⁰-occurrence DAG on which every closure runs exactly once per distinct subexpression; and chains 10⁵ deep. Tests never `assert_eq!` a large or shared expression, because printing a wrong one can take exponential time. `tests/interner.rs` runs alone in its own process and checks that the whole interner empties and shrinks.
