# cas-core

Hash-consed symbolic expressions for the CAS engine. The design is in Lific page `CAS-DOC-3`.

## Layout

| Module | Contents |
|---|---|
| `expr` | `Expr`, the 16-byte handle: a `Symbol` or an `i64` inline, or a shared interned node. Equality is O(1) (value or address) because every value has one representation. `Expr::apply`, `Expr::string`, `From<Symbol>`, `From<i64>`, `From<Integer>`, `From<Rational>` and `From<Complex>` (re-exported from cas-num): an integer that fits `i64` is always inline, a larger one an `Integer` node, a non-integer a `Rational` node, a rational with denominator 1 an integer, and a complex number a `Complex` node only when it is not real, so each number has one form. `view()` and accessors (`as_i64`, `to_integer`, `to_rational`, `to_complex`, `is_number`); cached metadata (`depth`, `size`, `may_contain` Bloom test, `is_numeric`, `has_pattern`); a structural hash. |
| `build` | The builder, the public way to construct expressions from Rust: `+ - * /` and unary `-` on `Expr`, `&Expr`, `Symbol` and `i64`, the assignment operators, `pow`, `Sum`/`Product` over iterators, `Expr::call`, `symbols`, `list`, `i` (the imaginary unit as a number) and `sin`/`cos`/…. Context-free: builtin `Flat` heads are flattened (iteratively) and `Orderless` heads sorted; nothing is evaluated (`x + x` stays `Plus[x, x]`). `x - y` is `Plus[x, Times[-1, y]]`, `x / y` is `Times[x, Power[y, -1]]`, and negating a number atom gives the negated literal (`-i64::MIN` is the integer `2^63`). |
| `symbol` | `Symbol`: a permanent `u32` id into a global, append-only name table, which starts with the builtins. |
| `builtin` | The builtin symbols (`Symbol::PLUS`, `Symbol::BLANK`, …): fixed ids `0..COUNT` and fixed attributes, declared in one list. Every name is an ASCII capital, then ASCII letters and digits, so the language's escaped user names (`$E`) never meet one. `I` is the imaginary unit's symbol, a constant that evaluates to the complex number, and `Complex` the head of complex numbers in full form. The list also holds the heads the language lowers to (CAS-120): comparisons and logic, `If`, `Function`, `Range`, `At`, the infinities, the statement heads `Set`, `SetDelayed`, `UpSetDelayed`, `Assume` and `Clear`, and the type tests of patterns; most of their evaluation comes with the Rewriting tickets. CAS-54 adds `SameQ`, `FreeQ`, `Length`, `Part`, `Map` and `Apply`; CAS-58 the sign predicates `Positive`, `Negative`, `NonNegative` and `NonPositive`, `Abs`, and the number sets `Integers`, `Rationals`, `Reals` and `Complexes`. Which heads are pattern constructs. |
| `attributes` | `Attributes`, the attribute bit set (`FLAT`, `ORDERLESS`, `HOLD_ALL`, `NUMERIC_FUNCTION`, …). |
| `assume` | Assumptions and the number domain (CAS-DOC-4 §7, CAS-58). `assuming` reads each assumption into facts once, evaluating the differences of its comparisons in the parent: comparisons, `Positive` and its kin, `And` and `Not` of them bound a *subject* (an expression as `scale·subject + shift` with the subject monic, so `x > y + 1`, `2 x - 2 y > 2` and `y - x < -1` all bound `x - y`), `Unequal` excludes a value, `Element` gives a number set, and anything else holds as written. A `Range` is what is known of a value: real or not, bounds with exact rational ends, not zero: from the facts about its subject and its structure (numbers are points, `Pi` and `E` lie between rationals, sums add ranges, coefficients scale them, products and powers combine signs, `Abs` is not negative, `Exp` of a real is positive). In the Real `Domain`, the default, symbols without facts are real. `Context::decide` (`Truth`) and `Context::sign` (`Sign`); a comparison reads the range of the difference of its sides, evaluated when neither is a rational, so `x + 1 > x` holds. Sound and cheap, not complete. |
| `context` | `Context`: values, rules, attributes of user symbols, assumptions (as written, and their facts), the number domain (`set_domain`) and limits. Values, attributes and rules are persistent maps keyed by symbol (`symbol_map`), so a clone is O(1) and a change to a shared state copies O(log n) of one map; `assuming` returns a child scope. `set` refuses a symbol with up-rules (`ContextError::HasUpRules`, O6), and `set_attributes` recompiles the rules that depend on the symbol (`rules`). Every state has a globally unique `StateId`, which keys the per-node fixpoint stamp (`is_fixpoint`, `mark_fixpoint`). `Limits`: `recursion` (512 by default, safe on a 2 MiB stack), `iterations` (4096), `match_steps` (10⁶ steps per match attempt, then `EvalError::MatchLimit`), `stack` (0, the caller's thread), `number_bits` (2²⁴: a power of numbers estimated larger stays unevaluated, and so does a polynomial builtin whose powers' coefficients would pass it) and `terms` (10⁶: the polynomial builtins leave a call whose products could pass it); `Limits::deep()` asks for a thread of its own with 256 MiB of stack and the recursion it holds (about 8.7·10⁴ levels). |
| `eval` | `Context::eval` (CAS-DOC-4 §2, CAS-53): numbers and strings are themselves and a symbol with a value its value; a node stamped as a fixpoint of the state, or met before in the call (the memo, keyed by node identity), is done at once; otherwise the head and the arguments not held (`HoldFirst`, `HoldRest`, `HoldAll`) are evaluated, `Sequence` arguments spliced (in held positions too), a `Flat` head flattened, a `Listable` head threaded over lists (lists of different lengths, or a list with a scalar, are `EvalError::Shape`: no broadcasting, O8), and an `Orderless` head's arguments sorted (in `Times`, lists keep their order after the other factors). Then the first rewrite source applies (R10): the up-rules of the arguments, the head's native function (`NativeFn` for a builtin, `UserNative` for a user's symbol), the system rules of a builtin or the rules of a user's symbol (exact ones first), and for a compound head its sub-rules and a `Function` (`rules`). A symbol with a value is replaced by it, and `I` has the value `i`. A change starts over and counts against `Limits::iterations`; a fixpoint is stamped. Statements (`Set`, `SetDelayed`, `Clear`) stay unevaluated. `Eval`, the state of one call, is what native functions receive: `eval` for parts, `context`, `warn`, and in a trace `step` (`trace`). Each nested evaluation is a level of `Limits::recursion`; a level takes at most `STACK_PER_LEVEL` (3072) bytes of stack (measured through arguments, heads, natives that evaluate their arguments, the matcher in order, as a multiset and in runs, rules whose conditions recurse, and a matrix power, which evaluates its products, without a trace and with one: at most 2848 in debug, 794 in release), so `RecursionLimit` comes before an overflow. Definitions, `matches` and `replace_all` run on the evaluation thread too (`run`). A `Function` head is applied in step 6 (`pattern::apply_function`). `Times` and `Power` are not `Listable`: `*` is the product of linear algebra (R13). |
| `native` | The natives of the builtins (CAS-DOC-4 §6.1, CAS-54), each receiving the normalised application. `Plus` adds its numbers, collects like terms in one pass (they are adjacent in the canonical order), drops zeros and unwraps one term; `Times` multiplies its numbers, adds the exponents of equal bases as far as the value is kept (`x x^(-1)` is 1; what O7 reads as a real odd root of a possibly negative base merges apart from the principal branch, and never out of it, CAS-154), multiplies powers of numbers with one exponent (`2^(1/2) 3^(1/2)` is `6^(1/2)`), makes `0 x` 0, and multiplies lists as in linear algebra, as Xcas does (`linear`, CAS-62): a scalar scales every entry, two vectors give their dot product, a matrix times a vector, a vector (as a row) times a matrix and two matrices give the matrix product, several lists fold from the left (`u*v*w` is `(u·v) w`), and dimensions that do not fit are `EvalError::Shape`; lists that are neither vectors nor matrices stay. A square matrix to an integer power from 0 is the matrix power, by squaring (the inverse waits for Linear algebra; a vector has no power). `ElementwiseTimes` (`.*`), `Listable`, multiplies entries. `Power`: exact numbers to integer powers (estimated results over `Limits::number_bits` stay), `x^0`, `x^1`, `1^x`, `(b^p)^n` and `(x y)^n` for an integer `n`, `(b^p)^q` for a positive rational `b`. Rational powers of rationals (`radical`) take a canonical form: a rational coefficient times powers of positive integers with exponents in (0, 1), one per exponent, perfect powers taken out per prime by trial division below 2¹⁶ and perfect-power tests (`12^(1/2)` is `2·3^(1/2)`, `2^(-1/2)` is `2^(1/2)/2`, `(2/3)^(1/2)` is `6^(1/2)/3`), square roots of negatives imaginary, other roots of negatives left for the number domain (CAS-58). `Sqrt[x]` is `x^(1/2)`. Infinities (`number::Infinity`): `DirectedInfinity` normalised to a direction of modulus 1 (`Infinity` for 1, `ComplexInfinity` for none), absorbing finite terms, turned by a coefficient's sign, and `Indeterminate` for `∞ - ∞`, `0·∞`, `0^0`, `∞^0`; `1/0` is `ComplexInfinity`. Comparisons decide on numbers, `±Infinity` and structurally equal expressions, and otherwise as the assumptions and the domain tell (`assume`); so do `Positive`, `Negative`, `NonNegative`, `NonPositive` and `Element`. `SameQ`, `FreeQ` and the type tests always decide: `IntegerQ`, `RationalQ`, `RealQ` and `ComplexQ` are true of a value the assumptions put in the set, `NumberQ`, `ListQ`, `MatrixQ`, `StringQ` and `SymbolQ` are structural. `Abs` of numbers (a complex one's is the square root of its norm), of infinities, of known signs, and of numeric coefficients. In the Real domain, odd roots of negatives are real (`(-8)^(1/3)` is -2, O7), `(b^p)^q` is `b^(p q)` for a real `b`, an integer `p` and an odd denominator of `q`, and `Abs[b]^(p q)` for an even `p` (`(x^2)^(1/2)` is `Abs[x]`); for `b >= 0`, `b^(p q)` in either domain; a directed infinity times factors of known sign turns by it; `And` and `Or` short-circuit, `Not` and `If` choose. `Length`, `Part` (0 the head, negative indices from the end), `Map` and `Apply`. `ReplaceAll` (from the top, a rewritten part not looked into again), `Replace` (the whole expression), `ReplaceRepeated` (until nothing changes, each round counted against `Limits::iterations`) and `MatchQ` compile their rules on each call, in the evaluation's context. In `Times`, patterns that match only lists (`_List`, `?MatrixQ`) keep their place with the lists, so a rule for `y x` keeps its order. `Plus` and `Times` first check, without building anything, whether the application is already normal, the usual case; debug builds check every such answer against the full computation. |
| `native::polynomial` | The polynomial builtins of phase 1 (CAS-DOC-8 §10, CAS-140), over `poly` and cas-poly, with Xcas's names in the language: `Expand` (`expand`), `Together` (`normal`), `GCD` and `LCM` (`gcd`, `lcm`; of numbers as numbers, `gcd(1/2, 1/3)` is `1/6`), `PolynomialQuotient`, `PolynomialRemainder` and `PolynomialQuotientRemainder` (`quo`, `rem`, `quorem`), `Exponent` (`degree`), `Coefficient` (`coeff`), `LeadingCoefficient` (`lcoeff`), `Numerator` and `Denominator` (`numer`, `denom`), `Content` and `PrimitivePart` (`content`, `primpart`), `Resultant` and `Discriminant`. `Expand` multiplies out products and positive powers, term by term (each term over the expansion of its own denominators, never a common one: `1/x + 1/(x+1)` stays), inside function arguments too, as giac; `Together` cancels, expanded, with the denominator's leading coefficient positive in the main variable, the last in canonical order (§15 detail 5). gcds include the gcd of the contents and are normalised (`gcd(x^2 - 1/100, x - 1/10)` is `10 x - 1`), `lcm` is expanded. The variable is the one given or the one generator with a symbol (§15 detail 6); with several, or when the input is not a polynomial in the given one, the call stays with a warning that says why. Division is over the fractions of the other generators (`quo(x^2, y x + 1, x)` is `x/y - 1/y^2`); by 0, the quotient is `ComplexInfinity` and the remainder `Indeterminate`. `degree(0)` is `-Infinity` (§15 detail 7); `coeff(p, x)` is the list of coefficients from the highest power, as giac; `resultant(c, b)` is `c^(deg b)` for a constant `c`. `Expand`, `Exponent`, `Coefficient` and `LeadingCoefficient` take balls as coefficients; the others leave the call with a warning (§9). A conversion past `Limits::terms` (10⁶ by default) leaves the call with a warning that gives the limit: each product is bounded before it is taken by the least of its products of terms, the monomials within its degrees and those within its total degree, so a dense product is judged by its result (`(1 + x + y + z + t)^20` squared: 113 million products of terms, 135751 terms); and an exact algorithm that would go dense in a variable of that degree or more stays too (CAS-156): gcds and `normal` in a variable two polynomials share, division by a polynomial in the variable, resultants and discriminants in any, contents in the other variables, and the list of coefficients, so `gcd(x^(10^8) - 1, x^(10^6) - 1)` warns where it ran out of memory, while `normal(x^(10^8) + 1)` and `quo(x^(10^8), 3)` answer at once; a power whose coefficients would pass `Limits::number_bits` stays too, by `e (log2 |c| + log2 t)` for the `e`-th power of `t` terms (CAS-156), and a number to a power that evaluation left is a generator, not a number to compute; the interrupt stops the long loops. Each call is one `Algorithm` step; `Trace::verify` checks `Expand`'s and `Together`'s and leaves the others, formal, unchecked. |
| `pattern` | Patterns and rules (CAS-DOC-4 §3, CAS-55). `Pattern::new` compiles `Blank`, `BlankSequence`, `BlankNullSequence`, `Pattern`, `Alternatives`, `Optional` (with the defaults of `Plus`, `Times` and the exponent of `Power`), `Condition`, `PatternTest` and `HoldPattern` into a tree of match nodes; anything else, and any part without a pattern construct, is a literal. It numbers the variables (slots), records the literal symbols every match contains as a Bloom filter in the format of the node metadata (so a subject without them is rejected at once), and lists the user heads it mentions, whose attributes matching depends on. The matcher backtracks through continuations, so every way of matching is tried, in order, until one completes: sequences shortest first, alternatives from the first, a repeated name bound to the same expression (O(1)); each node takes a level of the recursion limit while it is on the stack. The attributes a head has when the pattern is compiled decide how its arguments match (CAS-57): in order; under `Flat` in runs, a run of several bound wrapped in the head; under `Orderless` as a multiset (`multiset`), the arguments taking subjects in the order literals, fixed heads, blanks, applications a lone expression may stand for, sequences, a node whose value is known (a literal or a variable bound already) taking only an equal subject, equal subjects tried once, the last node taking what is left; under both, groups of several. With `OneIdentity`, a lone expression stands for an application whose arguments may all be missing but one (`a_. x_` matches `y`, `x_^n_.` matches `y`). In `Times`, lists are taken in the pattern's order. `Pattern::with_context` and `Rule::with_context` use a context's attributes and defaults, `Pattern::new` and `Rule::new` the builtins' only; definitions and the `Replace` natives compile in the evaluation's context. One match attempt takes at most `Limits::match_steps` steps (`EvalError::MatchLimit`). `substitute` instantiates a right-hand side but never inside a `Function` that binds a variable, so closures keep their parameter. `Rule::new` compiles `lhs -> rhs` once for reuse; a right-hand side `Condition[rhs, test]` is checked inside the match, so a failing test backtracks to the next way of matching. `Context::matches` and `Context::replace_all`. `apply_function` applies `Function[List[x, …], body]`, `Function[x, body]` and slot functions `Function[body]` (`Slot[n]`, not inside a nested slot function). `Pattern::with_defaults` takes the defaults of user heads, and `Pattern::filter` gives the dispatch filter of a rule (CAS-56). |
| `rules` | The rule store and dispatch (CAS-DOC-4 §5, CAS-56). Each symbol has its `SymbolRules`: an exact table for ground left-hand sides, keyed by the left-hand side with its arguments evaluated and normalised (`f[1 + 1] = 3` is the rule for `f[2]`; the arguments of an `Orderless` head sorted); patterned down-rules in definition order, where a rule with the same left-hand side and condition up to the names of its variables replaces the old one in place; the up-rules it carries for other heads, tied to an atomic argument, an argument's head or a blank's head (`f /: g[_f]`); sub-rules for `f[…][…]` on the innermost head; a native function; and the default of `x_.`. `Context::define` (`Set`: the right-hand side evaluated now), `define_delayed`, `define_many` (a library, all or nothing, on one evaluation thread), `define_up`, `define_up_delayed`, `clear` (attributes and default stay), `define_native` and `set_default`; protected symbols take none, a symbol with a value takes no up-rule and one with up-rules no value (O6). Each patterned rule has a filter on its subject's head, number of arguments and first argument, read from the compiled pattern. Changing the attributes or the default of a symbol recompiles the rules that mention it, found through an index: their stored left-hand sides are sorted and flattened again without evaluating, and definitions that become equal collapse as if made again in order. The system rules of the builtins are one static set, built on first use from `SystemRule`s, each with a stable name and the place it is written (`#[track_caller]`): those of `D` (`calculus`). |
| `calculus` | `D` as system rules (CAS-DOC-4 §8, CAS-59), the acceptance test of the rewriting design, so `D[x^2 + 3 x, x]` is `3 + 2 x` with no simplification asked for. In order: what is free of `x` has derivative 0 (`FreeQ`, the Bloom filter of the symbols), `x` has 1; lists thread and sums are mapped term by term at once (`Map`), so linear; the product rule `D[a_ b__, x_]` through AC matching; `f^n` with `n` free of `x`, `E^g`, `f^g`; the chain rule from a table (`Sin`, `Cos`, `Tan`, `Exp`, `Log`); a function the table does not know as `Derivative[1][g][u] D[u, x]`, and `Derivative[n + 1][g][u]` for `Derivative[n][g][u]`; `D[f, {x, n}]` for an integer `n >= 0`, and `D[f, x, y, …]`. A variable that is not a symbol leaves `D` as it is. Each rule is a `SystemRule` with a stable name (`"D/product"`), which a trace shows with the line it is written on. |
| `symbol_map` | `SymbolMap`, a persistent 32-way trie keyed by symbol id: a lookup follows one path, and an update copies the nodes on its path only where they are shared. |
| `order` | The canonical order, `impl Ord for Expr`: numbers first, integers of any size and rationals by value, then complex numbers by real part and imaginary part; products and powers compared as products of powers (factors from the last, then the coefficient), giving `1 + 2 x + x^2 + 2 y + 2 x y + y^2`; then strings, symbols by name (case-insensitive, lowercase first; a `$`-escaped user name, `$E` for the language's `E`, sorts as its spelling, just before it) and applications by head, length and arguments. Independent of ids, hashes and addresses. |
| `poly` | Expressions as polynomials and rational functions (CAS-DOC-8 §2, CAS-139), over cas-poly. `Polynomials::new` converts evaluated expressions over one list of generators: symbols, constants and every part that is no polynomial operation, in decreasing canonical order, so that `x₀` is the main variable of CAS-DOC-8 §15 detail 5. Roots split as giac writes them (`x^(3/2)` is `x · x^(1/2)`, `x^(2/3)` is `(x^(1/3))^2`). Exponentials of one base share a generator when their exponents are rational multiples (`E^(2x)` is `(E^x)^2`), the exponent taken as a constant and rational multiples of units over ℚ, as evaluation adds the exponents of powers of one base: sums split, multiples of sums distribute, complex multiples give `u` and `i u`. `Exp` stays whole. Each expression is a `Fraction`, a numerator and a denominator over ℤ, or ℤ\[i\] when a number is complex, its denominator's leading coefficient normalised; the arguments of a sum or a product are combined in a balanced tree, so a sum of `n` terms takes `O(n log n)` work (CAS-157: 135751 terms in 0.6 s, where adding each to the sum so far was quadratic); the lcm of two denominators that are not constants is `TooLarge` past the terms limit in degree, as its gcd goes dense (CAS-156); `CoefficientDomain` says ℤ, ℚ, ℤ\[i\] or ℚ(i). Balls, infinities, a zero denominator that evaluation left, a result past the `Bounds` on terms or on a power's bits (`Limits::terms`, `Limits::number_bits`) and the interrupt are `PolyError`s; `Polynomials::with_balls` takes balls as generators instead, for arithmetic. `polynomial_expr`, `fraction_expr` and `ratio_expr` build the way back, a constant denominator divided into the coefficients, for evaluation to make canonical, and `expanded_expr` each term of a numerator over its denominator. `expand` reads expressions as giac's `expand` does (CAS-140): only positive powers multiply out, every other power and `Exp` is a generator, and each term goes over the expansion of the product of its denominators. |
| `print` | Printing. `Debug` and `Expr::full_form` give the unambiguous full form, `Plus[x, Times[2, Power[y, 3]]]`; `Display` gives infix, `x + 2*y^3`. Infix parenthesises only where precedence needs it, subtracts terms with a negative coefficient, makes negative integer exponents a denominator (`x/(y*z^2)`), never lets an operand start with a sign after an operator (`2*(-3)`, `(-3)^2`, `x^(-1)`, `-(-x)`), and keeps `Times[-1, 3]` apart from the integer `-3` (`-1*3`). A rational prints `3/2` (full form too) and binds like a quotient (`x^(1/2)`, `2*(3/4)`, `1/2*x`); `Times[3, Power[2, -1]]` prints `3*2^(-1)` so it cannot be read as the rational. A complex number prints `2 + 3*I`, `3*I` or `I` and binds like that sum, product or atom (`x*(2 + 3*I)`, `(3*I)^2`, `x - I`); in full form it is `Complex[2, 3]`, apart from the symbol `I`. Both printers run on an explicit stack, so any depth prints in constant native stack. |
| `read` | `Expr::parse_full_form`, the inverse of the full-form printer (CAS-52): `head[args…]`, compound heads, integers of any size and reduced rationals, strings with the printer's escapes (`\"`, `\\`, `\n`, `\t`, `\r`, `\u{…}`), symbols whose names are identifiers (a letter, `_` or `$`, then letters, digits, `_` and `$`, so `$E` reads back), and `Complex[re, im]` of two numbers as the complex number. Whitespace between tokens is ignored, nothing is evaluated or normalised, and errors (`FullFormError`) carry a byte position. It runs on an explicit stack, so any depth reads in constant native stack. `assert_eval!(ctx?, input, expected)` states evaluator tests in full form (CAS-DOC-4 §10). |
| `trace` | The evaluation report (CAS-DOC-4 §9, CAS-61). `Context::eval_with(e, &EvalOptions { trace })` returns the result and a `Report`, also when evaluation fails: the `Warning`s, each once in order (`1/0` is `Infinite`; `0^0`, `0·Infinity`, `Infinity - Infinity` and `Infinity^0` are `Indeterminate`; a user's symbol with down-rules applied where none matches is `NoDefinitionMatches`, O2 and §5.3; natives add their own with `Eval::warn`; a message writes out an expression of at most 1000 parts, and of a larger one its head), and at `TraceLevel::Rules` or `Verbose` a `Trace`. A `Step` says that `before` became `after` at a `position` (the `Part` convention) and what did it, its `StepKind`: a rule by its `RuleId` (the symbol, the kind of rule, its place in definition order, and its `Origin`: a user's, or a system rule's stable name and place in the sources through `#[track_caller]`), a native function, a step a native reported with `Eval::step`, the memo (with the id of the first step of the earlier evaluation), normalisation (Verbose), an evaluation that a native or a condition asked for, with its steps as children (conditions at Verbose), and the rules tried that did not apply (`Attempts`, Verbose). The steps are a flat list in the order taken that name their parent and children by id, so a trace as deep as evaluation goes drops, clones and prints without recursion. At Verbose the top steps, replayed from the input, give the result. `Trace::verify` checks each step at exact sample points (integers, rationals or Gaussian rationals, as the facts and the domain say) that satisfy the assumptions mentioning its symbols, and gives the first step whose sides differ, with the point, and those it could not check. Without a report (`Context::eval`) the evaluation loop is an instance without the tracer's branches. A part whose evaluation warned is neither stamped nor memoised, so evaluating it again warns again. |
| `traverse` | Structural traversal, never evaluating. `part`, `Index` and `replace_part` use the `Part` convention (`0` is the head, `i ≥ 1` the arguments). `positions`, `pre_order` and `post_order` visit every occurrence. `distinct`, `symbols`, `contains`/`free_of`, `replace` (top-down), `substitute` (simultaneous literal rules) and `map_bottom_up` visit each distinct subexpression once, memoised by node identity, so they are linear in distinct subexpressions on shared DAGs. Unchanged parts keep their handle. `contains` and `substitute` skip subtrees by their metadata. All of it is iterative. |
| `node` | Interned nodes (integers outside `i64`, non-integer rationals, strings and applications) with their hash and metadata. Dropping releases children iteratively through a per-thread queue, so deep expressions use constant stack. |
| `intern` | The global interner: 64 mutex-protected shards of weak references, looked up with a borrowed key (a hit allocates nothing). A node removes its own entry when dropped, matched by address; shards shrink when sparse. `interner_stats()` reports occupancy. |

## Testing

Unit tests are in `src/tests.rs`. They cover structural equality against a plain tree model (proptest, with big integers, rationals and complex numbers among the leaves), one form per number at the `i64` boundaries whatever the constructor and for complex numbers with a zero imaginary part, sharing, entry removal, the builtin table and the `NUMERIC`/`HAS_PATTERN` flags (also against the model), constant-stack drops 10⁶ deep, and concurrent construction, churn and symbol creation. The rare interleavings (full hash collisions, a dead and a live entry for the same contents) are set up deterministically on a private shard. Context tests are in `src/context/tests.rs`: protection, scopes that don't leak, ids unique across threads, and a proptest that a stamp matches only the state that wrote it. Order tests are in `src/order/tests.rs`: polynomial and name examples, independence from creation order, and proptests of antisymmetry, transitivity and `Equal` exactly for equal expressions. Builder tests are in `src/build/tests.rs`: every operand type, constant-stack flattening 10⁵ deep, and proptests that `Plus`/`Times` match a flatten-and-sort model, are idempotent and ignore argument order, and that the operators agree with `Expr::call`. Printing tests are in `src/print/tests.rs`: precedence snapshots (negative numbers, nested unary minus, quotients, big integers and rationals, full-form fallbacks, string escapes), constant-stack printing 10⁵ deep, and proptests. Those parse the infix output back with a strict test parser, which rejects an operand that starts with a sign, and check that printing again gives the same text and the same numeric value. They also check full form against a recursive model. Reader tests are in `src/read/tests.rs`: symbols, numbers at the `i64` boundaries, complex numbers and the applications of `Complex` that are not numbers, every escape, compound and non-symbol heads, whitespace, every error with its position, text 10⁵ deep, `assert_eval!`, and proptests that full form reads back as the expression it was printed from (with or without whitespace between tokens) and that any text either fails cleanly or reads as something that reads back. Evaluation tests are in `src/eval/tests.rs`: atoms and values, holds, statements left unrun, `Sequence`, `Flat`, `Listable` (and its shape errors), `Orderless` (lists in `Times`), native functions and the evaluation of their results, the memo (a repeated part evaluated once per call), stamps (a second evaluation calls nothing), the iteration and recursion limits on the caller's thread and on a thread of its own (10⁵ levels), the stack each level takes through arguments, heads, natives, the matcher, rules and a matrix power against `STACK_PER_LEVEL`, without a trace and with one (run in debug and release), and proptests of idempotence, of results with and without stamps and memo, and of argument order under `Orderless` heads. `benches/poly.rs` (CAS-141) times the polynomial builtins through evaluation, conversion and the way back included: `Expand` of `(1 + x + y + z)^K` (5.1 ms at `K = 20`, 1771 terms) and of Fateman's `f (f + 1)` at `K = 5` and `10` (64 ms, 10626 terms), `Together` of `N` fractions `1/(x + k)` (0.24 ms at `N = 20`) and of `N` terms that cancel (0.77 ms); the ignored test `poly_workload` of `src/native/polynomial/tests.rs` is the same for instruction counts (`CAS_CORE_POLY=expand-fateman:20`: 152 G instructions, 10 s, nearly all cas-poly's product). `benches/calculus.rs` times `D` of polynomials of 10 to 10⁴ terms (linear, about 10 µs a term), of `Sin` nested 10 to 300 deep (about cubic: each level sorts its factors again, and the canonical order compares `Cos[Sin[…]]` down their nesting) and of products of 10 to 100 factors (tending to quadratic: the product rule splits off one factor at a time). `benches/eval.rs` times evaluating a sum of 10³ to 10⁵ terms (linear, about 6.7·10⁶ terms/s), evaluating the result again (22 ns at every size: the stamp), and evaluating the sum with a trace at the Rules level (`traced`: 10 to 15% slower, with little to record). Report tests are in `src/trace/tests.rs`: tables of traces as trees (what took each step: values, exact, down-, up- and sub-rules, natives and `Function`, a builtin's value, a native's evaluations as its children, the memo's pointer; what Verbose adds, normalisation, conditions and attempts; what Rules leaves out, conditions and what sources that did not apply evaluated), a native's own steps and warnings, the warnings (each kind, each once and in order, again on a second evaluation), the report of a failed evaluation (the steps in progress close), `verify` (a wrong rule located, points that satisfy the assumptions, integer points, the domain, large powers left alone at points, what it cannot check), messages that leave a large expression out, and a proptest that a Verbose trace replays to the result and each evaluation recorded to its value, on random expressions with rules of every kind, conditions, natives that evaluate and repeated parts (4000 cases; 2·10⁵ in release). Native tests are in `src/native/tests.rs`: tables of sums, products, powers (within `Limits::number_bits`), radicals, infinities and the singular cases, comparisons and predicates, logic and conditionals (with a test that short circuits evaluate nothing more), and structure; proptests that arithmetic on numbers agrees with cas-num, that rational powers keep their numeric value in a form that evaluates to itself, that perfect powers come out, and that the order of the arguments of `Plus` and `Times` changes nothing; and what the evaluator fuzz target (`fuzz/`, CAS-60) found, each input in its context. Calculus tests are in `src/calculus/tests.rs`: tables (the acceptance test, the table and the chain rule, products, quotients, powers, unknown functions, higher and partial derivatives, lists, variables that are not symbols), a trace that names the rules where they are written, and proptests that `D` of a random polynomial is its closed form, that `D` of a random rational function at a random rational point is what forward differentiation over exact rationals gives there (the product, quotient, power and chain rules), and that `D` of a chain of table functions agrees with the product of the derivatives along it at a random rational point. Tests of products of lists are in `src/native/linear/tests.rs`: tables checked against giac 1.9 (dot products, both sides of a matrix, `A B` against `B A`, rectangular shapes, the fold from the left, scaling, symbolic entries, powers, entrywise sums and `.*`), every dimension error, the unevaluated entries a product builds (a scalar reaches each entry of a matrix), and proptests that matrix products are associative on random matrices of fitting sizes and that a power by squaring is the repeated product. Pattern tests are in `src/pattern/tests.rs`: tables for blanks and bindings, sequences (fewest first, bindings that must agree across nested parts, splicing), alternatives, `Optional` and the builtins' defaults, conditions, tests, `HoldPattern`, `Replace`, `ReplaceAll` and `ReplaceRepeated` (with a right-hand condition that backtracks), `Function` with names and slots; closures that keep their parameter; a compiled rule reused across contexts against compiling it again; malformed patterns; and proptests that the matcher finds a match exactly when a brute-force reference does, with the same first bindings, and that the symbol filter changes no result. Tests of `Flat`, `Orderless` and `OneIdentity` matching are in `src/pattern/multiset/tests.rs`: tables for each, the lists of `Times`, rules on such heads through dispatch, the budget (deterministic, `MatchLimit`), pruning that keeps easy matches within a small budget, and a proptest against a brute-force oracle over pattern expressions (no pruning, every copy of equal subjects tried) on existence and on the first match, half of the cases from patterns made from their subjects so that a third match. `benches/matching.rs` times linear cases (a split, a literal removed, a fixed head among 10⁴ terms: 35 to 50 ns per term) and the exponential one, three sequences under an `Orderless` head (about ×10 for two more arguments), up to where the budget stops it (12 arguments: 0.19 s, about 190 ns per step). Assumption tests are in `src/assume/tests.rs`: tables in both domains (signs, real roots, powers of powers, `Abs`, `Element`, the type tests, bounds and their implications, facts about differences in any form, exclusions and number sets, negated facts, assumptions that hold as written, scopes and the API, rule conditions that see the facts, the ranges of balls, by their exact ends or, past `2^16` bits, by their signs, CAS-171), and a proptest that `decide` and `sign` never contradict exact evaluation at points that satisfy random assumptions (bounds of either strictness, exclusions, both sides), in both domains. Rule tests are in `src/rules/tests.rs`: exact keys evaluated and normalised, definition order after the exact rules, replacement in place by key (variable names, conditions, literals that share a variable's name), `Set` against `SetDelayed`, recursion through rules on the caller's thread and on a thread of its own, sub-rules, up-rules before natives and on blanks' heads, the O6 exclusions, protection, `clear`, user natives, defaults, recompilation after an attribute or default change (rules of `g` and `h` that mention `f`), held left-hand sides, `define_many` all or nothing, persistence, malformed definitions and the system rules; proptests that setting attributes after the definitions gives the rules and the results of setting them before, and that the dispatch filters change no result. `benches/rules.rs` times dispatch through 10 to 10⁵ exact rules (flat: 0.11 µs, 1446 instructions, per evaluation), through 10 and 100 patterned rules that the first-argument filter tells apart (0.22 and 0.40 µs: about 50 instructions per rule skipped) or that the matcher must try (0.57 and 4.0 µs: about 38 ns per attempt), loading 10³ to 10⁵ definitions (linear: about 0.18 µs per exact definition and 1.1 µs per patterned one, and O(log n) more when every state is kept, 1.4 to 2 µs), and evaluating a sum of 10⁴ terms with and without an up-rule elsewhere (0.75% more with one: an application whose Bloom filter has none of the tags skips up-rules in O(1)). The map has a proptest against a `BTreeMap`, with a snapshot that later changes must not reach. Traversal tests are in `src/traverse/tests.rs`: proptests against recursive models (orders, distinct, positions and parts, `contains`, `symbols`, `replace`, `substitute`, `map_bottom_up`); a 2¹⁰⁰-occurrence DAG on which every closure runs exactly once per distinct subexpression; and chains 10⁵ deep. Conversion tests are in `src/poly/tests.rs`: CAS-139's table of generators and domains (`sin(x)^2 - 1`, `e^x` with `e^(2x)` and with `e^(x/2)`, `Exp` apart from `E^x`, `x` with `sqrt(x)`, `sqrt(2)`, `f(x)`, `x^n`, `x^(10^30)`, roots of one order and of two, symbolic multiples, bases apart, constants, a number inside a generator); fractions (sums over the lcm of the denominators, a normalised denominator, roots and exponentials as powers of their generators, generators shared by several expressions); the errors (a ball, infinities, the terms and exponent limits, a zero denominator that evaluation left, the interrupt); the way back in canonical forms; the cases where evaluation adds exponents, which the round trip found; and proptests on random sums of products of numbers, variables, powers, roots, exponentials and sines: without roots the way back converts to the same rational function, twice over; with roots it keeps the value at points where the roots are exact; and polynomial expressions keep their values at rational points; the bound of a product's terms is at least its terms, and exactly them for dense polynomials; and a sum of 30276 monomials converts within a loose time bound, which the quadratic sum missed by far. Polynomial builtin tests are in `src/native/polynomial/tests.rs`: tables of every builtin from giac's answers (CAS-DOC-8 and more), with the departures of §15; `expand` term by term; held arguments; the calls that stay, each with its warning (an inferred variable among several, not a polynomial in the variable, balls in the exact algorithms, infinities, a constant's discriminant); the terms limit, tight for dense and sparse products, and the dense forms it refuses at high degrees, with those it needs not; the interrupt, before a call and inside a product; one `Algorithm` step per call, the traces of every builtin verified; and proptests that `Expand` of a product is cas-poly's product, as a sum of monomials, and that `Together` of a sum of fractions keeps its value at rational points, expanded, with a numerator and denominator that cas-poly finds coprime (20000 cases each). Tests never `assert_eq!` a large or shared expression, because printing a wrong one can take exponential time. `tests/interner.rs` runs alone in its own process and checks that the whole interner empties and shrinks.
