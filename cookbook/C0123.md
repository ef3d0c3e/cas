# C0123 — giac sqff takes x^v out first: where the sign of a negative lead goes
tags: giac,sqff,sturmseq,sign,caseval,factor · date: 2026-11-28 · phase/task: 2.1/T12 · origin: CAS-661 CI 46047327203

giac's square-free factorisation (sqff, used by `sturmseq`/`sturm` and likely other factor-based natives) takes `x^v`
out before Yun's split, so the sign of a negative leading coefficient lands on a factor of `p / x^v`, never on a
factor that is `x` alone, unless `v = 1` and the rest has no odd factor (then `-x` carries it). Otherwise giac keeps
`-1` apart as its own factor: `sturmseq(-x^3)` = `[1,[-1,0],[[1,0],1],1]`, `sturmseq(-x^3*(x-1))` puts it on `1-x`,
`sturmseq(-x^3*(x-1)^3)` on the merged `x-x^2` (x joins the rest's factor of multiplicity v).
How it was found: random sweeps rarely draw a negative monomial; when one disagrees, probe a family with
`PY scripts/caseval.py` (negative monomials, `-x^k*f^m` for k,m in 1..5, with v = 1 and v > 1) and fit the rule
before you choose between a fix and a departure. Fix in cas-core `native/polytools/roots.rs` `parts()` (CAS-661).
