# Certified complex roots by real Krawczyk on the 2n real system

To certify roots of an analytic f(x) (or a square system) over ℂ with a real-only Krawczyk test, reuse it on the 2n
real system: x_k = a_k + i b_k, F = (Re f, Im f), evaluated numerically at the complex point (no symbolic Re/Im
splitting). The real Jacobian comes from the complex one by Cauchy–Riemann: [[Re J, −Im J],[Im J, Re J]], J by `D`.
In cas: `System { complex }` with `substitution`/`values`/`jacobian_at` in
crates/cas-core/src/native/solve/fsolve/systems.rs, used by `newton` and `krawczyk` alike; `certified(ev, fs, vars,
start, complex)`; cfsolve in solve/fsolve/complex.rs (T5, CAS-536).

- Newton from a real start on a real-coefficient function stays on the real line: retry once off the axis
  (start + i/2 per unknown) when the real start does not certify; without a start, use a fixed non-real start.
- When every imaginary ball holds 0, certify again on the real system and return a real ball.
- Candidates from a closed form (CSolve's roots, evaluated) beat blind starts: they find every principal root.
- Polynomials in one unknown skip all this: `polynomial_roots` (cas-num) isolates every root at once.
